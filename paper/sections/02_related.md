## 2. Related Work

<b>Agent interoperability protocols.</b> The Model Context Protocol defines how tools and context are exposed to models [@anthropic2024mcp]; Agent2Agent defines how agents discover and message each other [@google2025a2a]. Surveys of inter-agent trust compare identity, claims, proofs, stakes, and reputation [@hu2025interagent]. GCP is a vertical commerce protocol that runs over such transports and adds commerce-specific objects, safety rules, and authorization.

<b>Agent identity and payment.</b> Poppy (a personal-agent protocol announced in November 2026) provides cryptographic identity, delegation, and session access [@poppy2026]; UCP and ACP focus on catalog, checkout, and payment [@ucp2026; @acp2026]. GCP treats these as profiles: Poppy supplies the principal and delegation; UCP/ACP supply generic commerce primitives; GCP adds vehicle context, safety gating, after-sales, and a delegation ladder. Our earlier work studies incentive design for long-lived agents [@su2026incentive]; this paper studies the protocol layer that makes those incentives enforceable in automotive commerce.

<b>Contracts and incentives.</b> Principal–agent theory explains why hidden effort and hidden information produce distorted behavior [@holmstrom1979moral; @holmstrom1991multitask]. Recent work applies contracts to LLM delegation [@saig2024incentivizing; @sreenivas2026contracting; @velasevic2026anytime] and mechanism design to LLM outputs [@duetting2023mechanism]. GCP's authorization ladder is a practical contract: it makes budget, scope, and human step-up explicit and auditable.

<b>Automotive safety and software standards.</b> ISO 26262 defines functional safety for road vehicles; UN R155 and R156 define cybersecurity and software-update requirements; ECE-R90 regulates replacement brake linings. GCP does not replace these standards; it encodes their commerce-facing consequences (OEM approval, recall status, certification) as protocol checks and evidence.

<b>Human versus automated commerce.</b> Human online shopping has well-known error and return costs; automation reduces search and execution time but can amplify errors when constraints are hidden. Our analysis separates two effects: <i>information</i> (the protocol makes constraints machine-checkable) and <i>agency</i> (the agent acts without continuous human attention). The benchmark is designed to measure both.
